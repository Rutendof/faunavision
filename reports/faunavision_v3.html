FaunaVision · v301 · Summary
01 · Executive summary

From a species demo
to a tool rangers can use

Camera traps in Southern African reserves produce millions of photos. More than half are empty, and the rest are sorted by hand, often months late. FaunaVision filters out the empty frames, identifies the species (including notoriously similar lookalikes), flags priority sightings such as rhino, and runs offline at a field station.

Version 2 of this blueprint described a fine-grained classifier on a generic iNaturalist subset. Version 3 keeps its scientific core (CNN vs Vision Transformer, metric learning, hierarchical prediction, explainability) and points it at a real conservation problem, with real camera-trap data and a test protocol that reflects how the tool would actually be deployed.

2.17Mlabelled camera-trap images from 12 Southern African reserves
54%of those images are empty frames: the waste triage removes
64classes in 23 lookalike clusters, 11 flagged as priority

FaunaVision · v302 · The problem
02 · The real-world problem

Half the photos are grass
moving in the wind

Camera traps are one of conservation's most important monitoring tools, and one of its biggest data bottlenecks.

A single reserve can run dozens of cameras, each triggered by heat and motion. Wind, shadows and sun on grass trigger them as reliably as animals do. Teams of staff and volunteers then look through every frame by hand.

The consequences are practical: poaching evidence is found weeks late, rare species sightings are missed, and population surveys stall because nobody has time to label the images.

Species identification is the second bottleneck. Many reserves hold lookalikes that are hard to separate on a blurry infrared frame: white vs black rhino, plains vs mountain zebra, blue vs black wildebeest, the small antelopes. Volunteers often record these simply as "rhino" or "small antelope", losing exactly the detail conservation needs.

FaunaVision · v303 · System
03 · How the system works

Four stages, from memory
card to ranger report

STAGE 01

Detect

MegaDetector, a free open model built for camera traps, finds animals, people and vehicles, and discards empty frames.

IN: raw frames
OUT: boxes or "empty"
STAGE 02

Classify

Our fine-tuned model identifies the species from a square crop around each animal, at species, genus and family level.

IN: animal crops
OUT: ranked species
STAGE 03

Triage

Calibrated confidence decides: auto-accept, human review, or priority alert (rhino, carnivores, people at night, livestock).

IN: predictions
OUT: queues and alerts
STAGE 04

Report

Per-camera summaries, explanations for each decision, exportable CSV. Runs offline on a field laptop.

IN: decisions
OUT: ranger report

What the user sees

  • Batch mode: point the tool at a folder from a memory card and get back a sorted, labelled set with empty frames removed.
  • Priority feed: rhino, wild dog, lion, leopard, cheetah, elephant, hyenas, and livestock or people inside the reserve, surfaced first.
  • Lookalike explanation: for any close call, a side-by-side view showing which body parts separate the top two candidates.
  • Review queue: uncertain frames go to a human, with a genus or family-level answer ("rhino, species unsure") instead of a guess.
  • Single-photo mode: the original v2 upload experience remains for demos and education.

06 · Models

A deep learning ladder,
every rung measured

No model is trained from scratch. Each starts from weights pretrained on millions of images and is fine-tuned on our crops, under identical splits and metrics.

6
Distilled student (ViT-S or EfficientNet-B0)

Learns from model 5, exported to ONNX for fast offline use.

Kaggle → laptop

Training recipe

  • Linear probe first, then full fine-tune (LP-FT)
  • AdamW, layer-wise LR decay ≈ 0.7, cosine schedule with warmup
  • Label smoothing 0.1, moderate augmentation, weight EMA
  • Mixed precision and gradient checkpointing to fit free T4 GPUs
  • Logit adjustment or class-balanced sampling for rare species
  • Three seeds for headline comparisons; test set used once

Experiments, one at a time

  • Metric learning (SupCon or ArcFace) for tighter species clusters and the similar-species gallery
  • Hierarchical heads for species, genus and family, with graceful fallback
  • Location and season prior from iNaturalist occurrence data
  • iNaturalist top-up: do clean photos help rare camera-trap classes?
  • Banner masking: remove camera logos and timestamps so the model cannot cheat
Free compute budget. Roughly 60–80 GPU-hours in total, or 2–3 weeks of Kaggle's free quota. Images are cropped once up front, features are cached, and checkpoints are saved every epoch so 12-hour session limits never lose work.

FaunaVision · v307 · Innovation and evaluation
07 · What makes it different

Four ideas, each
backed by a measurement

01 · CONTRASTIVE EXPLANATIONS

Why A, not lookalike B

Grad-CAM on the difference between the top two scores, plus DINOv2 patch matching against reference images, shows which body parts separate the candidates.

02 · UNSEEN-RESERVE TESTING

Honest generalisation

Pilanesberg, Kruger and Camdeboo never appear in training. Results show what a new conservancy can expect.

03 · CALIBRATED TRIAGE

Knows when it doesn't know

Temperature scaling, genus fallback and a review queue, tuned with risk–coverage curves so priority species are almost never auto-dismissed.

04 · PHOTO → CAMERA TRANSFER

Learning rare species from citizen science

Measures whether iNaturalist photos rescue classes with fewer than 100 camera-trap images, such as wild dog and cheetah.

Risks and responsible use

  • Sensitive locations. Rhino sightings with locations are valuable to poachers. The app never publishes coordinates, and demo outputs strip camera and location IDs.
  • Not a sole decision-maker. Predictions support people; they don't replace verification before any field action.
  • Closed species list. Animals outside the 64 classes may be mislabelled; the supported list is always visible, and low confidence routes to review.
  • Dataset bias. Reserves, seasons, camera models and day/night balance vary; results are broken down along these lines.
  • Licensing. Snapshot Safari (CDLA-Permissive) and openly licensed iNaturalist photos only; attribution kept; images not committed to the repository.
